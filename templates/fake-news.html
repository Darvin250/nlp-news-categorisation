What is Fake News?

Fake news is misinformation or disinformation presented as legitimate news to mislead readers. Below are short, practical examples showing a real news excerpt vs. a fake news example so you can compare signals and writing style.

How to spot fake news

  • Check the source and look for reputable outlets.
  • Verify claims with multiple independent sources.
  • Watch for sensational language and missing author/source information.
  • Look for supporting evidence such as links to original documents or data.

Examples: Real vs Fake

Real News Example

Headline: Government announces new economic policy to support small businesses.

In a press conference, the Minister of Finance outlined a package of measures including grants for micro-enterprises and tax credits for hiring initiatives. The announcement included links to the official policy document and statements from independent analysts.

Why this looks real: cites sources, uses measured language, provides links and quotes, and references verifiable documents.

Fake News Example

Headline: Miracle cure discovered doctors shocked as thousands heal overnight!

Social posts claim a single ingredient can cure multiple serious illnesses; the post contains no reputable sources, relies on anecdotes, and urges readers to "share immediately". Images are reused from unrelated events.

Why this looks fake: sensational claims, no sources, anecdotal evidence, and calls-to-action that amplify spread.
